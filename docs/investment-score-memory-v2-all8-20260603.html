🏗 HBF详细技术分析（来源：OCP提交文件，2026.02.25）
Here is a dense technical analysis of High Bandwidth Flash based on the latest available data through mid-2026. --- ## High Bandwidth Flash (HBF): Technology, Players, and AI Data Center Role ### What Is HBF? High Bandwidth Flash is a new memory class that stacks multiple 3D-NAND dies vertically, connects them through-silicon via (TSV), and integrates a controller base die with per-channel ECC, wear-leveling engines, and high-speed PHY circuitry at the bottom of the stack. The result is a NAND-based package that fits within the same physical footprint as an HBM4 stack and targets an order-of-magnitude higher capacity at comparable bandwidth. The term was coined by SanDisk (now independent from Western Digital) and the technology was formally announced at a joint SanDisk/SK Hynix event in Milpitas, California on February 25, 2026, where the two companies simultaneously submitted HBF to the Open Compute Project (OCP) for standardization. ### Specifications vs. HBM HBF Gen 1 targets 512 GB per 16-high stack at 1.6 TB/s read bandwidth, directly matching the bandwidth floor of HBM4, while delivering 8-16x the capacity of HBM4 (which maxes at ~48 GB per stack, or ~384 GB with eight stacks). Power envelope runs 20-80 W per stack, yielding >6.4 GB/s/W bandwidth efficiency and >6 GiB/W capacity efficiency. The main penalty versus DRAM-based HBM is latency: HBF carries ~10 microsecond random read latency at the page level, versus sub-100 nanosecond for HBM. Transfer granularity is also coarser, at tens of kilobytes per access. Gen 2 is projected to exceed 2 TB/s read and 1 TB stack capacity; Gen 3 targets 3.2 TB/s and 1.5 TB per stack. By contrast, HBF is more than 50x faster than top-tier PCIe 5.0 SSDs at a fraction of HBM's cost per gigabyte, positioning it squarely between HBM and NVMe in the storage-memory hierarchy. ### Company Landscape **SanDisk** is the primary architect of the HBF standard and has submitted the specification to OCP. First engineering samples are targeted for H2 2026, with AI inference system samples in early 2027. **SK Hynix** co-signed the OCP workstream launch on February 25, 2026, and simultaneously published the "H3" hybrid architecture paper (also appearing on IEEE Xplore) showing how HBF and HBM can be daisy-chained via the HBM base die into a unified address space. Here is the dense factual analysis you requested: --- **NAND Flash and Enterprise SSD in AI Data Centers: 2025-2028** **NVIDIA CMX: Rack-Scale KV Cache on NVMe** Originally announced as ICMS (Inference Context Memory Storage) at CES in January 2026 and rebranded to CMX (Context Memory eXtension) at GTC in March 2026, NVIDIA's platform directly addresses the GPU memory wall for long-context and agentic inference. CMX introduces a new "G3.5" storage tier — positioned between node-local NVMe (G3) and shared enterprise storage (G4) — built on BlueField-4 DPU-managed NVMe SSDs connected via NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet RoCE fabric. The BlueField-4 offloads KV cache data integrity, encryption, and NVMe-oF transport entirely from the host CPU. The software layer — DOCA Memos — exposes a key-value API consumed by NVIDIA Dynamo, the distributed inference-serving framework, enabling pod-wide KV cache reuse and request routing to where context already resides. Performance targets claim up to 5x higher sustained tokens-per-second for long-context workloads versus traditional storage approaches. CMX-based platforms began shipping in the second half of 2026. Solidigm (SK Hynix subsidiary) is the named SSD partner, leveraging its 60 TB, 122 TB, and planned 244 TB QLC EDSFF drives optimized for this tier. **Per-Rack SSD Density: Quantifying the Demand** A single NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 rack — 72 Blackwell GPUs, 36 Grace CPUs, 130 TB/s aggregate NVLink bandwidth — carries approximately 16 TB of NVMe SSD per GPU, implying roughly 1,152 TB of NAND per rack. **AI Inference Memory Hierarchy Architecture 2026: NVMe SSD in the NVIDIA GB-Series Rack Era** --- **NVMe SSD Footprint per GB200/GB300 NVL72 Rack** Each NVL72 compute tray supports 8x E1.S NVMe drives (3.84 TB each, PCIe Gen 5 x4) plus a 1x 1.92 TB M.2 boot drive. Across the 18 compute trays in a single NVL72 rack, that yields approximately 553 TB of raw NVMe performance storage plus ~35 TB of OS boot capacity — totaling roughly 587 TB of NVMe per rack (Lenovo Press, Supermicro datasheets). The GB300 NVL72 carries identical tray storage architecture, inheriting the same per-rack NVMe footprint while upgrading GPU compute to B300 silicon. These drives are attached via integrated NVMe storage controllers, not a shared SAN fabric, making them truly local to each tray. Against this, the same rack holds 13.5 TB of HBM3e (192 GB per B200 GPU x72) and 2.25 TB of LPDDR5X across 36 Grace CPUs — meaning NVMe SSD already outruns on-package DRAM by roughly 37:1 in raw capacity per rack, a dramatic inversion from CPU-server norms. **NVIDIA CMX / ICMSP: Formalizing NVMe as Inference Memory** At CES 2026, NVIDIA announced the Inference Context Memory Storage Platform (ICMSP), now branded CMX, which standardizes KV cache offload from HBM through host DRAM and onto NVMe SSD. Powered by BlueField-4 DPUs and the NIXL (NVIDIA Inference Xfer Library) block transport protocol, CMX manages tiered KV cache movement off the GPU compute path, keeping hot context in HBM and spilling cold context to NVMe. NVIDIA claims CMX delivers up to 5x improvement in tokens-per-second throughput and up to 5x power efficiency versus traditional storage approaches (NVIDIA Newsroom, January 2026; Solidigm ICMSP page).
