Here is a dense technical analysis of High Bandwidth Flash based on the latest available data through mid-2026. --- ## High Bandwidth Flash (HBF): Technology, Players, and AI Data Center Role ### What Is HBF? High Bandwidth Flash is a new memory class that stacks multiple 3D-NAND dies vertically, connects them through-silicon via (TSV), and integrates a controller base die with per-channel ECC, wear-leveling engines, and high-speed PHY circuitry at the bottom of the stack. The result is a NAND-based package that fits within the same physical footprint as an HBM4 stack and targets an order-of-magnitude higher capacity at comparable bandwidth. The term was coined by SanDisk (now independent from Western Digital) and the technology was formally announced at a joint SanDisk/SK Hynix event in Milpitas, California on February 25, 2026, where the two companies simultaneously submitted HBF to the Open Compute Project (OCP) for standardization. ### Specifications vs. HBM HBF Gen 1 targets 512 GB per 16-high stack at 1.6 TB/s read bandwidth, directly matching the bandwidth floor of HBM4, while delivering 8-16x the capacity of HBM4 (which maxes at ~48 GB per stack, or ~384 GB with eight stacks). Power envelope runs 20-80 W per stack, yielding >6.4 GB/s/W bandwidth efficiency and >6 GiB/W capacity efficiency. The main penalty versus DRAM-based HBM is latency: HBF carries ~10 microsecond random read latency at the page level, versus sub-100 nanosecond for HBM. Transfer granularity is also coarser, at tens of kilobytes per access. Gen 2 is projected to exceed 2 TB/s read and 1 TB stack capacity; Gen 3 targets 3.2 TB/s and 1.5 TB per stack. By contrast, HBF is more than 50x faster than top-tier PCIe 5.0 SSDs at a fraction of HBM's cost per gigabyte, positioning it squarely between HBM and NVMe in the storage-memory hierarchy. ### Company Landscape **SanDisk** is the primary architect of the HBF standard and has submitted the specification to OCP.
